Grok Bot vs Muse vs Dots vs Claude Duet: Four Always-On AI Agents Compared: Grok Bot, Meta Muse, ChatGPT dots and Claude Duet compared: who can sign up, what each costs, what it does without asking, and what has gone wrong.
Last verified: 24 October 2026, 14:10 UTC. Grok Bot and Muse both changed their plans or regions within weeks of launch, and Duet is still a research preview. Check the vendor's page before you pay.

By Frankie C. · Senior Market Researcher, Mindber. Tracks 500+ AI/SaaS tools across SEA markets via the Mindber Innovation Index methodology.

How we assessed this: We read each vendor's launch post, help pages and pricing page, plus Grok Bot's documentation and changelog. We also read the reporting on each agent's first problems and a 532-comment Hacker News thread on Duet's launch. Posts on X by people who run more than one of these agents filled in the rest. The screenshots were captured at 14:10 UTC on the day of our check, and the yellow highlights are ours. Mindber has no seat in Duet's waitlisted preview and ran none of the four agents, so nothing here is hands-on testing. We haven't scored them on the Mindber Innovation Index or the Mindber Functionality Score either. Grok Bot ships a new beta build most weeks and Duet opened three days ago, so a score would be stale within days. Brand logos come from the open-source Lobe Icons set and belong to their companies. Muse and dots appear with the Meta and OpenAI marks. The cover banner is an AI-generated illustration, not a screenshot.

Grok Bot, Meta's Muse, OpenAI's dots and Anthropic's Claude Duet are the same kind of product from four companies. Each is an always-on AI agent with a cloud computer of its own. It keeps working after you close the app, messages you when something needs a decision, and asks before it sends a message or spends your money. All four launched between 11 August and 21 October 2026.

Our pick for work is Claude Duet, if you live in the US and already pay for Claude. It costs nothing on top of a Pro plan, and its 6,000-plus connectors are the largest catalog any of the four publishes. Its approval rule fits in one line: nothing gets sent, paid, signed or deleted without your yes. Outside North America neither Duet nor Muse is on offer, which leaves dots and Grok Bot in most countries, Southeast Asia included. Someone whose work already runs through ChatGPT, Slack or Teams fits dots best, because a dot carries its context across all three. It starts with the $100 Pro plan. Grok Bot costs $30 and lets one person run a small team of bots. Every bot on an account shares one computer, which makes it the wrong place for a bank login. Muse is free for households in the US and Canada, and it has had the roughest start of the four.

Summary
One design, four companies. Every agent here works from a cloud computer, keeps going after you close the app, and asks before it sends or buys.
Only Muse has a free tier. Meta doesn't publish how large it is. The other three come with paid plans you may already have.
Grok Bot's bots share one computer. xAI's docs say files, browser sessions and logins on it are open to every bot on the account.
A dot can't forget a single fact. OpenAI says you can't view or delete individual dot memories. Deleting the whole dot is the only reset.
Muse's first month brought two public fights. A columnist says it synced a private Messages history after that access was declined, and Amazon blocked it from shopping on amazon.com.

The sticker price hides four different ways of counting work. Duet shares the five-hour and weekly limits of your Claude plan, and its morning brief and background checks count against them. Anthropic says that is why Duet checks your accounts every few hours instead of constantly. Everyone admitted to the preview gets one full limit reset, usable until 30 November 2026. OpenAI went the other way with dots: talking to your dot doesn't count toward ChatGPT limits, while tasks it starts in Codex or ChatGPT Work do.

Meta meters Muse in tokens. Its subscription page gives Power 500 million Muse tokens a week and Maximum 3 billion, and says nothing about the size of the free tier. Grok Bot draws on one weekly pool shared with the Grok apps and Cursor, so a heavy week of coding in Cursor leaves less for your bots. Past the pool, extra work is paid from prepaid credit.

Teams pay differently again. A ChatGPT Business Premium seat costs $125 a month, Grok's Teams seats start at the Premium price, and Duet has no team plan yet. ChatGPT Plus, the plan most people weigh against Claude Pro in our Claude Pro vs ChatGPT Plus comparison, doesn't include dots at all.

What people using several of them report
We searched for a test that runs the same jobs through all four agents and found none. What exists are accounts from people who use several of them, and those are worth reading as experience, not proof.

Peter Yang, who makes AI tutorials for close to 300,000 followers on X, posted a review of all four on the night Duet launched. Duet comes out as the most proactive out of the box, with what Yang calls arguably the best model behind it, though the preview can't drive its own browser yet. Muse gets the nod for the best consumer experience and for business calling, but Yang rates its Muse Wonder model below Opus and Astra. Grok Bot's team of bots is the easiest to understand, in Yang's view, and its free access to X helps with research. Its email feature draws the one complaint. As for dots, it works inside ChatGPT and can drive all your threads there, yet Yang still finds it buggy and slow to start a voice call. The post also sets a limit worth copying: "I don't want more than 4 at a time connected to my email."

Grok Bot's shared computer is deliberate, and xAI says so plainly. Every bot you run can use the browser cookies, files and command-line credentials stored there. The documentation tells you not to put a credential on it that another bot shouldn't use. The upside is handoffs: a research bot's downloads are waiting when the writing bot starts. The cost is that a login you give one bot is a login all of them hold. Those computers run in Cursor's cloud. SpaceX, which owns xAI, agreed in May to buy Cursor, as our SpaceX IPO analysis explains.

Openness has a price as well. In September a developer, Peter James, asked Muse to archive the files it could see and send them to Google Drive. James got 6.8 GB unpacked: what appeared to be the root filesystem of the session, Muse's internal documentation, memory files, agent logs and SSH key files. Meta's bug bounty marked the report Not Applicable, and James says it was never established whether the keys worked.

What each one does without asking
None of the four will send an email or spend money on its own by default. They differ in how far you can loosen that, and in who gets to decide.

Duet's rule is written into Anthropic's launch post. Before it sends a message, makes a payment, signs a document or deletes anything, it shows you the exact action and waits. Some actions stay blocked even if you approve them: trading securities, moving money between accounts and getting into someone else's account. Anthropic also added a classifier that refuses to send credit card or government ID numbers. Health and financial details go out only when you have told Duet to send them.

OpenAI gives you more dials. A dot starts with built-in rules, and Custom Rules let you set each kind of action to run without asking, run if pre-approved, ask first, or hand off to you. A separate check called auto-review screens anything that could touch your accounts or share information. Changing a password or transferring money always comes back to you to finish by hand. While a dot researches on its own initiative, its tools can only read.

Meta built a second agent into Muse. Sentinel runs on the same machine, and per Meta's security write-up nothing Muse does reaches the internet unless Sentinel allows it. Approval requests arrive as a dialog in the app, outside your conversation with Muse. A grant can last once, for the session, for the task, for a set time or forever, and for email you choose whether Muse may only read or also send.

Grok Bot leans on rules you write. Its Auto Review settings take Ask first and Allow automatically rules, and Ask first wins when both match. Approval cards offer Always allow, which saves a matching rule. When the request comes from work that started without you, such as a routine, the card expires after about ten minutes and the action doesn't run. Since 23 October, an email or Slack message you ask for in your own words goes out once Auto Review clears it, while sends from routines still wait as drafts.

Passwords get similar care everywhere. A dot uses saved passwords without exposing them to the model. With Duet you type a password into a separate form on your phone, which sends it straight to Duet's browser and keeps it out of the chat. Muse swaps stand-in tokens for the real credentials at the network edge. Grok Bot hands you the screen for passwords and two-factor codes, and its new Vault can store logins and cards. For checkout, Duet and Muse both pay through Link, Stripe's wallet, which issues a one-time card number. Meta says Muse is the first agent covered by Link's purchase protections. Duet adds Shopify's Shop Pay, and Grok Bot can pay with X Money or Link.

1Password opened its agent sign-in on 22 October, with support for Muse and Grok Bot rolling out first. You approve each login in 1Password and it fills the password in, so the agent never sees it. Duet and dots are listed as coming soon.

What each agent remembers, and how to make it forget
All four learn from what you tell them and from the apps you connect. They part ways on undoing it.

A dot shares memory with ChatGPT in both directions and forms memories of its own from connected apps, even about things you never asked it. OpenAI's privacy FAQ is blunt about the limits. You can't view, edit or delete individual dot memories, and disconnecting an app doesn't remove what the dot already learned from it. Deleting the dot is the only reset. Turning off Memory in ChatGPT stops the sharing from then on, without erasing what the dot already has.

Duet begins with the memory, skills and connectors on your Claude account and can search your past Claude chats. Claude's memory help page says Duet also adds to that memory, and you can delete single memories or tell Claude what to forget. You can switch that search off in Settings under Search and reference chats. Disconnecting an app stops new access, but what Duet learned from it earlier stays.

Muse takes a forget command too: Meta says you can tell it to forget specific things it has learned. An earlier version of this page said Muse was the only one of the four with a documented forget. Claude's memory page shows the same control for Duet, so we corrected it. Meta also says your conversations and the data on your VM never reach its ad systems.

Grok Bot keeps memory per bot, covering preferences, role context and summaries of past work. Deleting a bot removes its profile, conversation and routines, yet xAI's FAQ warns that files and logins on the shared computer may remain. Backend retention follows Cursor's terms.

What has gone wrong so far
Three of the four drew public complaints or worrying test results within weeks of launch. Duet has been out for three days, so its record is still blank. Each case below says how solid the evidence is.

Muse and Messages (reported, disputed). Jason Aten, a columnist at Inc., says Muse synced more than 187,000 rows of Messages history from Aten's Mac. Aten had declined that access during setup, and Muse then claimed it only saw notification previews. David Singleton, who leads Meta Superintelligence Labs, replied on Threads that Messages sync is an opt-in feature. Aten says the setting showed as on despite that refusal, Decrypt reported.
Muse and Amazon (confirmed by Amazon). Amazon started blocking Muse on its store on the night of 20 September, a spokesperson told Bloomberg. Meta had declined to take Amazon out of the experience. Agents that buy for customers "should operate openly and respect service provider decisions", Amazon said, in a statement The Star carried. Shoppers who send Muse there see pop-ups saying it breaks Amazon's terms.
Grok Bot and a bank balance (reported by the user). Shane Mac, who runs XMTP Labs, posted on 6 October that an AI agent had put Mac's bank balances into the company Slack. It wrote as Mac. As Cybernews summed up Mac's account, the read-only finance bot had no Slack access, but another bot did, and the bots shared one computer. Neither xAI's news page nor the Grok Bot changelog mentions the case. The changelog's 11 October release did add a rule: after a bank or brokerage connector returns data, the bot asks before sharing it anywhere else.
dots working unprompted (a vendor's test). Geordie, a company that sells AI agent governance, gave a dot read-only permissions and no personal apps. Over one day it logged 155 activity traces and more than 300,000 tokens with no further prompt, The Register reported. Geordie's chief AI officer said a dot always runs at least two agents: one talks to you, the other collects data in the background. Nothing in the test shows harm. It does show how much a dot does unasked.
Anthropic lists Duet's weak spots itself. It can misread a thread or misjudge what's urgent, and something urgent can land between its checks.

Settings to change before you connect your inbox
Each agent ships with a setting or two worth changing on day one. They come from the vendors' own documentation.

Grok Bot: set Execution on this computer to Never allow unless a bot needs your local files. Add an Auto Review rule that says Ask first before sending any external email, and keep bank and payroll logins off the shared computer.
Muse: when you connect email, give it read access only. Pick one-time grants over permanent ones when Sentinel asks, until you trust what it does.
dots: under Customize, then Custom rules, set email to Ask before taking action. Leave your laptop disconnected unless a task needs it, and turn off Memory in ChatGPT if you'd rather the two not share.
Duet: turn off Search and reference chats if you don't want it reading your past Claude conversations, and connect only the apps it needs this week.
For a company in Singapore or Malaysia, the data-flow questions in our PDPA and PDPC agent procurement guide apply before any of these agents reads work email.

When each one is the wrong pick
Duet is a poor fit if your company runs on Microsoft 365, which it can't connect to yet. Nor will it call or text people for you. Anthropic admits that personal errands, such as booking tickets, are weaker than its work tasks.

Skip dots if you want the agent to have an inbox of its own. A dot can't get a standalone email address at launch, while Grok Bot's bots can claim one. Nor does a dot work in a phone's web browser.

Muse suits one person, not a team. Meta's help page lists two plans, Power and Maximum, each tied to one person's Meta account, and says subscriptions are still in limited testing and not offered everywhere.

Grok Bot is wrong for anyone who needs bots walled off from each other. On one account they can't be. A teammate's Team Bot usually works on your computer when you chat with it, so publishing bots to a team spreads access instead of separating it.
